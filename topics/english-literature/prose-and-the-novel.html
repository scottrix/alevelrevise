Prose and the Novel

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level All Boards (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC, CCEA) AQA

A-Level English Literature revision: Prose and the Novel. Learning objectives, key points, worked examples and practice questions across AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC and CCEA.

📌 Key Points

Key Fact: Character: direct/indirect presentation; protagonist/antagonist; character arc
Key Fact: Narrative perspective: 1st (intimate, biased), 3rd limited (one consciousness), omniscient (all-knowing), stream of consciousness
Key Fact: Setting: time, place, atmosphere; pathetic fallacy; symbolic settings
Key Fact: Themes: developed through motifs, recurring images, contrasts, parallels
Key Fact: Context: author's life, period (Victorian, Modernist, Postcolonial), reception history
Key Fact: Critical lenses: feminist, Marxist, psychoanalytic, postcolonial, eco-critical

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Analyse characterisation: flat/round, static/dynamic, foils
  • Understand narrative voice: 1st, 3rd limited, 3rd omniscient, unreliable narrator
  • Explore themes through plot, setting, and symbolism
  • Contextualise novels: historical, social, cultural, biographical
  • Evaluate critical interpretations and theoretical approaches

💡 Worked Example

Exam-Style Question

Question: Discuss how the first-person narration in 'The Great Gatsby' shapes our understanding of Gatsby

Model Answer:

Nick as participant-observer creates intimacy but limits access to Gatsby's interiority. His judgement 'Gatsby turned out all right' frames our view. Unreliable moments (drunk at party) and admiration create ambivalence. We see Gatsby through Nick's romanticisation
