Critical Writing and Context

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level All Boards (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC, CCEA) AQA

A-Level English Literature revision: Critical Writing and Context. Learning objectives, key points, worked examples and practice questions across AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC and CCEA.

📌 Key Points

Key Fact: PEEL: Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link back to question
Key Fact: Quote analysis: technique + meaning + effect + link to argument
Key Fact: Context: woven into analysis (AO3) not separate; historical, literary, biographical
Key Fact: AO5: alternative readings (e.g. feminist, Marxist); critical quotations as springboards
Key Fact: Comparison: integrated structure (point by point) not text by text; similarities/differences
Key Fact: Exam technique: plan 5 mins, write 45 mins, check 5 mins; answer the question set

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Structure thesis-led essays using PEEL/PEAL paragraphs
  • Select and analyse quotations effectively (not dumping)
  • Integrate context (AO3) without bolt-on paragraphs
  • Evaluate alternative interpretations and critical views
  • Write comparative essays across texts

💡 Worked Example

Exam-Style Question

Question: Write a thesis statement for: 'Explore how Shakespeare presents ambition in Macbeth'

Model Answer:

Shakespeare presents ambition as a corrupting force that destroys moral order, using Macbeth's hamartia and Lady Macbeth's manipulation to show how unchecked desire for power leads to tyranny and self-destruction, ultimately restored by divine retribution

Q3: Write a PEEL paragraph analysing how Shakespeare presents Lady Macbeth as a subversion of Jacobean gender expectations in Act 1, Scene 5.

Show Model Answer

Answer: Point: Lady Macbeth inverts patriarchal norms by rejecting femininity itself. Evidence: the imperatives 'unsex me here' and 'take my milk for gall' (1.5). Explanation: the metaphor of gall (bitterness, poison) replacing maternal nurture aligns her with the witches' androgyny and disturbs the natural order that James I prized, so ambition is presented as masculinising. Link: therefore Shakespeare invites his Jacobean audience to view her transgression of gender roles as a threat to divine and political order.

Q4: 'Context should be woven into analysis, not bolted on.' How far do you agree with this view of A-Level essay writing?

Show Model Answer

Answer: Woven context scores higher on AO3 because it shows contextual factors shaping meaning rather than listing them: explaining that Stevenson's 'Jekyll and Hyde' reflects Victorian anxieties about Darwinian degeneration deepens analysis of its split setting, whereas a paragraph beginning 'In the Victorian era...' detached from the argument adds little. However, brief explicit context can clarify for the examiner when integrated into the PEEL explanation stage. Judgement: context belongs inside analysis, but only when it advances the line of argument rather than interrupting it.

Q5: Evaluate a feminist reading of 'The Great Gatsby' that suggests Daisy Buchanan is a victim of patriarchal wealth rather than a mere antagonist.

Show Model Answer

Answer: Support: Daisy's voice 'full of money' objectifies her as an asset of Tom's estate; her cynicism ('beautiful little fool') shows awareness of limited female agency in the 1920s; her choice of security over love reflects constraints on women without independent wealth. Challenge: she remains complicit -- she kills Myrtle and lets Gatsby take the blame. Judgement: the feminist reading enriches interpretation (AO5) but overstates her passivity; Fitzgerald presents her as both victim and accomplice, so a balanced reading is more convincing than either pole.
