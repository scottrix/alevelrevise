Poetry: Pre-1900 and Post-1900 Anthologies

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC/Eduqas, CCEA

A-Level poetry revision for the AQA A (7712) love anthologies plus AQA B, Edexcel and OCR set poems: pre-1900 and post-1900 methods, unseen poetry technique and comparison structures that hit AO1-AO5.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Analyse how form, metre, rhyme and imagery shape meaning in the pre-1900 (Donne, Marvell, Hardy, Keats) and post-1900 (Heaney, Duffy, Larkin) anthologies (AO2).
  • Compare two anthology poems, or an anthology poem with your prose text, via linked topic sentences naming method and idea (AO1/AO4).
  • Apply a timed unseen-poetry method to the compulsory two-poem question (AQA A Paper 1 Section B, 25 marks).
  • Weave in context: courtly love, metaphysical wit, carpe diem, Victorian double standards, confessional poetry (AO3).
  • Evaluate alternative readings of poems and reach a supported judgement (AO5).

📋 Key Definitions and Core Concepts

Metaphysical conceit: Extended, intellectual metaphor yoking dissimilar things; Donne's flea fuses insect, marriage bed and temple - the argument, not the image, persuades.
Iambic pentameter: Five da-DUM units per line, the dominant pre-1900 line; substitutions flag emphasis or disturbance.
Carpe diem: Seize-the-day tradition urging the beloved to yield before time passes; Marvell builds his whole syllogism on it.
Dramatic monologue: One speaker, an implied listener, self-revelation unintended; Hardy's The Ruined Maid exposes the Victorian double standard.
Caesura: A pause within a line; Hardy's At an Inn uses hesitation to register social constraint.
Enjambment: Run-on lines carrying sense past the line-end; in post-1900 poems it mimics love overflowing formal containment.
Volta: The turn in a poem's argument, classically at sonnet line 9; Rossetti's Remember turns possession into release.
Free verse: Post-1900 abandonment of regular metre and rhyme; rhythm becomes deliberate evidence - Heaney's loose lines in Punishment.

📌 Key Points

AQA A (7712): you study ONE anthology - pre-1900 (14 poems, Wyatt to Dowson) or post-1900 (15 poems, Millay to Cope) - examined open book in Paper 1 Section C with your prose text.
Paper 1 Section B is two UNSEEN poems (25 marks) - a reusable timed method is non-negotiable.
AQA A-level AO weightings: AO1 28%, AO2 24%, AO3 24%, AO4 12%, AO5 12%.

Pre-1900 anthology: courtly love, metaphysical wit and Victorian doubt

The AQA A pre-1900 anthology runs from Wyatt's Whoso List to Hunt to Dowson's Non sum qualis eram bonae sub regno Cynarae - fourteen poems crossing courtly allegory, Renaissance wit, Romantic ballad and Victorian doubt. It is examined in Paper 1 Section C (open book), where anthology poems link to your prose text; the same period range drives OCR Component 01, and AQA B's tragedy anthology draws on Keats and Tennyson's Tithonus.

  • Courtly allegory: Wyatt casts pursuit as ownership - the deer 'wild for to hold, though I seem tame' wears Caesar's collar; desire is already property law.
  • Metaphysical seduction: Donne's The Flea builds a three-stanza syllogism (the flea bit you first; its body is our marriage bed; kill it and see) - the conceit argues, and the bluff ending shows wit courting defeat.
  • Carpe diem logic: Marvell's To His Coy Mistress pivots from conditional hyperbole ('vegetable love') to 'But at my back' and 'Now therefore' - time's chariot and the vault turn flattery into pressure.
  • Romantic ballad: Keats's La Belle Dame sans Merci uses quatrains with shortened final lines and circular structure, so landscape reads as psychology.
  • Victorian doubt: Hardy's At an Inn (dramatic irony - only onlookers believe the affair) and The Ruined Maid ('ruin' means prosperity) expose the sexual double standard; Rossetti's Remember makes the volta an act of release; Tennyson's Tithonus (AQA B) turns immortality without youth into love's nightmare - 'Me only cruel immortality'.

A* habit: pair each method with its ideology. The flea is not decoration but a patriarchal argument; the ruined maid's dialect is not local colour but economics. Revise by tradition, not by poem, so any pairing finds you with two ready frameworks.

Board mapping
  • AQA A 7712: anthology in Paper 1 Section C, open book; unseen pair in Section B.
  • AQA B 7717: tragedy anthology includes Tithonus and the Keats ballad tradition.
  • OCR H472: pre-1900 poetry sits in Component 01 alongside drama.
  • Edexcel 9ET0: comparison skills transfer to Poems of the Decade (AO4).

Post-1900 anthology: modern love and fractured forms

The post-1900 anthology runs from Millay's I, being born a woman to Cope's After the Lunch. Modern love poems inherit old forms but hollow them out: Millay wears the Petrarchan sonnet ironically; Duffy cites the tradition in order to fail it.

  • Heaney, Punishment: the bog body of an executed adulteress draws tenderness ('My poor scapegoat') against complicity ('I almost love you / but would have cast... the stones of silence'). Free verse, archaeological metaphor and apostrophe let admiration and judgement coexist in one voice.
  • Duffy, The Love Poem: a collage of love-poetry echoes that keeps failing to complete itself; fragmentation and enjambment enact the claim that tradition cannot say love whole.
  • Larkin, Talking in Bed and Wild Oats: anti-romantic, colloquial deflation; intimacy becomes distance, and love shrinks to a keepsake photo.
  • Sexton, For My Lover, Returning To His Wife: confessional mode and mythic register give jealousy a public voice the pre-1900 beloved never had.
  • Jennings, One Flesh: aged marriage as 'chastity' - distance measured in silence and separate books.
PairComparison point (AO4)
The Flea / Sextonseduction by wit against seduction by rage - who gets a voice?
At an Inn / Talking in Bedpublic judgement versus private silence as causes of distance
Remember / A quoi bon direthe same loss decades apart - closed couplet confidence versus free-verse question

Comparative insight for A*: the shift is not form alone but who is granted interiority - the pre-1900 beloved is an object of argument; post-1900 poems give the woman (Millay, Sexton, Duffy) the argument itself.

Poetic methods and the unseen poem

Method checklist for any poem: form (sonnet, ballad, monologue, free verse); metre (pentameter, tetrameter, substitutions); rhyme (couplet, ABAB, half-rhyme, refrain); imagery (conceit, sensuous, natural); voice (courtly address, confessional, conversational); structure (volta, syllogism, circularity, fragmentation).

Unseen method (AQA A Paper 1 Section B - two unseen poems, 25 marks, roughly 60 minutes):

  1. Read both poems twice: first for situation, second for method. Fix speaker, addressee, and any gap between them.
  2. Annotate form, imagery, structure; mark short quotable phrases.
  3. Find the point of contact: a shared theme and a shared or contrasting method.
  4. Write four paragraphs - one method per paragraph, applied to BOTH poems, hinge ('whereas', 'while') inside the topic sentence.
  5. Conclude by weighing: which poem achieves more, and why - commit to a view.
Topic-sentence stems that earn AO4 credit
  • Both poets use X, whereas the effect differs because...
  • Where Poem A formalises grief through..., Poem B lets form break down...
  • The anthology poet's compression makes the idea harsher than the novel's mediation.

For Section C, remember genre is a method: a poem's compression against a novel's narrative voice is itself an AO2/AO4 argument. Keep quotations to single phrases - open book rewards precision, not transcription.

💡 Worked Example

Exam-style: AQA A (7712) Paper 1 Section C style (25 marks, open book): 'In love poetry the beloved is always a fiction the lover constructs.' Compare how poets from your anthology and the author of your prose text present the gap between desire and the person desired.
Mark-scheme logic: Mark-scheme logic: AO1 (28%) coherent comparative thesis with sustained judgement; AO2 (24%) methods and effects in every paragraph across both texts; AO3 (24%) contexts woven, not bolted; AO5 (12%) readings weighed. Thesis (AO1): pre-1900 poets construct the beloved through inherited convention - Wyatt's deer and Donne's flea are arguments, not people - while post-1900 poetry exposes the construction: Heaney's speaker admits the bog woman exists chiefly as his projection. Paragraphs (AO2) isolate one method each - conceit, monologue, free verse, voice - and apply it to both texts. Conclusion (AO5): weigh whether modern texts demystify the beloved or install a new fiction; commit.

❓ Practice Questions & Mark Schemes

Paper 1 Section B style (25 marks): compare two unseen poems on lost love, focusing on how voice and structure present grief.
Answer: Read twice; fix speaker, addressee, situation. Thesis: Poem A grieves through formal control (regular quatrains, closed couplet) so grief is mastered; Poem B grieves through breakdown (free verse, broken syntax) so grief is relived. Paragraphs: voice (public elegy vs private address); imagery (seasonal decay vs physical absence); structure (volta as acceptance vs repetition as haunting); sound (closed rhyme vs unresolved half-rhyme). Conclusion: weigh - control distances feeling, breakdown includes the reader - then judge which is the more honest elegy.
'The argument of The Flea is comic bravado rather than coercive pressure.' In the light of this view, consider how Donne presents seduction.
Answer: AO5: argue both readings before choosing. Bravado: the conceit is a game; the syllogism ends in anticlimax; the speaker concedes with 'Just so much honor... Will waste, as this flea's death took life from thee'. Pressure: the argument denies her authority over her own body, folds consent into theology ('three lives in one flea spare') and brands refusal cruelty ('Cruel and sudden'). Judgement: Donne dramatises seduction as a contest the woman wins, which complicates - not cancels - the coercion reading.
Compare how Hardy's At an Inn and Larkin's Talking in Bed present emotional distance in love.
Answer: Thesis: Hardy's distance is socially produced - onlookers misread the pair, so propriety makes intimacy impossible; Larkin's is structural - the couple grows 'more distant' the closer they lie. AO2: Hardy's dramatic irony versus Larkin's flat declaratives and landscape similes; closed Victorian rhyme versus unresolved half-rhyme. AO3: Victorian propriety versus post-war disillusion. AO4/AO5: same theme, opposite causes - weigh which poem is the more damaging diagnosis and commit.
'Heaney's Punishment is an apology that becomes another judgement.' How far do you agree?
Answer: AO5-led. For: apostrophe ('My poor scapegoat') pities, but 'I almost love you / but would have cast... the stones of silence' confesses he would have joined the executioners; 'your betraying sisters' spreads the judgement outwards. Against: the poem self-indicts - the speaker's 'artful voyeur' implicates the gaze itself. AO2: loose free verse, bog-as-archive imagery, caesuras at moments of doubt. Judgement: the poem shows judgement is unavoidable - even tenderness takes sides.
