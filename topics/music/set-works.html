Set Works (Edexcel Areas of Study)

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level Edexcel, OCR, WJEC/Eduqas

A* revision of the Edexcel 9MU0 set works across all six Areas of Study - vocal, instrumental, film, popular/jazz, fusions and new directions - with key features, context and exam technique for Section A and Section B.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Identify the key musical features, context and language of each set work across the six Edexcel areas of study (9MU0/03) (AO1/AO3).
  • Analyse set-work extracts from audio with skeleton scores, naming devices (leitmotif, ostinato, idée fixe, chorale) and their effects (AO4).
  • Place each set work in its genre and historical context - Lutheran cantata, programme symphony, film score, 1960s pop, acid jazz, gamelan influence, spectralism, primitivism (AO3).
  • Draw links between set works and unfamiliar music in the Section B essay one (20 marks) (AO4).
  • Evaluate the musical elements, context and language of one set work as a whole in essay two (Q6, 30 marks) (AO3/AO4).

📋 Key Definitions and Core Concepts

Idée fixe: Berlioz's recurring melody representing the beloved, transformed in each movement of Symphonie Fantastique.
Leitmotif: A short recurring musical idea bound to a character or idea - the organising principle of Elfman's Batman Returns and Herrmann's Psycho cues.
Ostinato: A persistently repeated rhythmic or melodic figure; Herrmann's Prelude drives on one, Debussy layers them in Pagodes.
Cantus firmus: A fixed melody, often in long notes, around which other parts are woven - the chorale in augmentation in Bach's Ein feste Burg movement 1.
Pentatonic scale: A five-note scale (e.g. C-D-E-G-A) central to Javanese gamelan and to Debussy's Pagodes.
Habanera: A slow Cuban dance in duple metre with a dotted (dotted quaver-semiquaver) dotted rhythm and syncopation underpinning La soirée dans Grenade.
Spectralism: A late-20th-century approach (Saariaho) where timbre, spectra and the interior of sound itself, not melody or metre, shape the music.
Primitivism: Early modernist use of raw rhythmic energy, dissonance and folk/ancient material - Stravinsky's The Rite of Spring (1913).

Vocal and instrumental set works

Bach, Cantata Ein feste Burg ist unser Gott BWV 80 (movements 1, 2, 8). A Lutheran Reformation cantata on Psalm 46, its tune attributed to Luther. Movement 1 is a chorale fantasia: the chorale melody appears in augmentation (long notes) in the unison violins while the bass voice and continuo state it in canon; voices enter imitatively over driving independent instrumental lines. Movement 2 is a duet for soprano and bass in chorale-prelude texture, the chorale sounding in long notes (organ pedals) beneath more florid voices. Movement 8 is the closing four-part chorale: the congregation's hymn, harmonised in Bach's style - root-position cadences, suspensions, stepwise bass. Context: Lutheran worship, chorale as congregational spine, Word over word painting. Exam angle: cadences, modulations to closely related keys, suspensions, canon and augmentation.

Vaughan Williams, On Wenlock Edge (Nos. 1, 3, 5). A song cycle for tenor, piano and string quartet setting Housman's A Shropshire Lad. No. 1 'On Wenlock Edge' opens with a storm depiction (tremolando, sweeping glissandi in the strings); No. 3 'Is my team ploughing?' is a dramatic dialogue between a dead man and his living friend, with sharply contrasting piano and string answers; No. 5 'Bredon Hill' evokes church bells (pealing open fifths) and a serene pastoral ending. Context: English pastoral school, Tudor-inflected harmony, 1909 (revised later). Exam angle: word painting, texture between piano and strings, modal inflections, sonority.

Clara Wieck-Schumann, Piano Trio in G minor Op. 17, movement 1. Sonata form: agitated G minor first subject (chromatic, often compared to Bach's Musical Offering theme), flowing second subject in the relative major B-flat; development with fugal writing; coda. Context: 1846, her only piano trio, written by a woman composer in a male-dominated profession. Exam angle: sonata form in a minor key, chromatic third relations, chamber texture and dialogue between piano and strings.

Berlioz, Symphonie Fantastique, movement I (Réveries - Passions). Programmatic, semi-autobiographical (Harriet Smithson), 1830. Long slow introduction; allegro with chromatic first subject, transition, late lyrical second subject (the idée fixe), development and a coda recalling the idée fixe before a chorale-like close. Expanded orchestra, extreme dynamics, programme over Classical balance. Exam angle: how Berlioz bends sonata form; idée fixe; orchestration as narrative.

Film and popular/jazz set works

Danny Elfman, Batman Returns (Birth of a Penguin Parts I and II, Batman vs the Circus, The Rise and Fall from Grace). Gothic orchestral score (1992): dark chromatic harmony, minor-mode leitmotifs for Penguin and Catwoman, circus-band grotesquerie, low brass and celesta colour, driving ostinati under sweeping melodic lines. Elfman's language: diatonic melody over chromatic, often tritone-related harmony; abrupt cues cut to picture. Exam angle: leitmotif transformation, orchestration, harmonic colour, music's role in characterisation.

Bernard Herrmann, Psycho (Prelude, The City, Marion, The Murder (Shower Scene), The Toys, The Cellar, Discovery, Finale). Written for string orchestra alone (1960) - a deliberate choice for uniform timbre. 'Prelude' runs on relentless rhythmic ostinato; 'The City' dashes in fast homophony; 'The Murder' deploys shrieking high-register violin stabs, minor seconds and tritones - melody abandoned for pure dissonance; 'The Toys' and 'The Cellar' keep tension with fragmentary gestures. Exam angle: ostinato, dissonance (clusters, minor 2nds), string-only sonority, how cues mirror Hitchcock's editing; black-and-white minimalism.

Kate Bush, Hounds of Love (Cloudbusting, And dream of sheep, Under ice). 1985 art-pop, self-produced on the Fairlight CMI sampler. 'Cloudbusting' (based on Peter Reich's memoir of his father Wilhelm Reich) builds from hypnotic cello ostinato and a rolling drum pattern to a soaring chorus (with David Gilmour's guitar); 'And dream of sheep' is a fragile live-piano lullaby of a drowning sailor; 'Under ice' uses icy synth pads, echoing voice and the Fairlight's sample manipulation for claustrophobia. The last two sit inside the 'Ninth Wave' concept suite. Exam angle: production techniques (sampling, layering), structure and build, text painting, art-pop context.

Beatles, Revolver (Eleanor Rigby, Here, there and everywhere, I want to tell you, Tomorrow never knows). 1966 studio revolution. 'Eleanor Rigby': double string octet (George Martin arrangement), no conventional pop backing, Dorian-mode inflected verse, loneliness theme. 'Here, there and everywhere': gentle 1960s ballad with jazz-inflected harmony and shifts. 'I want to tell you' (Harrison): Indian-inflected, tension between melody and dissonant pedal. 'Tomorrow never knows': one-note (C) drone, tape loops, backwards guitar, ADT vocals, Tibetan Book of the Dead inspiration - the avant-garde absorbed into pop. Exam angle: instrumentation vs genre expectations, studio techniques, modal and drone harmony, 1960s cultural context.

Courtney Pine, Back in the Day (Inner state (of mind), Lady Day and (John Coltrane), Love and affection). 2000 UK acid-jazz/fusion: jazz saxophone (Pine on soprano and other saxes) over club-culture grooves (drum'n'bass, hip-hop beats, scratching), synthesised textures, 'Lady Day and (John Coltrane)' paying tribute to the jazz tradition within modern production, 'Love and affection' reworking Joan Armatrading. Exam angle: fusion of jazz harmony/improvisation with popular grooves, sampling, cultural context of British Black music.

Fusions and new directions set works

Debussy, Estampes (Pagodes, La soirée dans Grenade). 1903. 'Pagodes' translates Javanese gamelan (heard at the 1889 Paris Exposition) into piano music: pentatonic melody, layered ostinati, heterophonic doubling, bell-like sonority, sustained resonances - Western harmony suspended. 'La soirée dans Grenade' fuses Spanish colour: habanera bass rhythm, modal melody, hints of bitonality, guitar-imitating figuration. Exam angle: pentatonicism, layering, non-functional harmony, musical exoticism and its context (colonial-era Paris, Exposition Universelle).

Anoushka Shankar, Breathing Under Water (Burn, Breathing Under Water, Easy). 2007 (with Karsh Kale): Indian classical meets electronica/pop. Raga-based sitar melody, tanpura drone, tabla tala cycles and improvisatory phrasing, layered over programmed beats, loops, synths and English-language vocals; 'Easy' adds pop-song verse-chorus shape. Exam angle: raga/tala vs western metre and tonality, drone, texture fusion, world-music collaboration context.

Kaija Saariaho, Petals for Violoncello and Live Electronics. 1988. Spectralism: timbre and colour as the primary musical material - harmonics, sul ponticello, tremolando, col legno and other extended techniques; live electronics transform and resonate the cello in real time; non-pulsed, floating time replaced by density of texture rather than metre; harmony derived from spectral thinking rather than functional progression. Exam angle: extended techniques, the role of technology, timbre as structure, late-20th-century context.

Stravinsky, The Rite of Spring (Introduction, The Augurs of Spring, Ritual of Abduction). 1913, premiered to riot at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées. 'Introduction': high solo bassoon (Lithuanian folk-inflected melody) above whispering strings - ritual dawn. 'Augurs of Spring': the famous pounding chord (a dominant 7th superimposed on a major chord a semitone below) struck on off-beats with irregular accents - primitivist rhythm replacing metre; 'Ritual of Abduction' accelerates into frenetic, fragmented ostinati. Exam angle: rhythmic displacement and metrical disruption, dissonant superimposition, ostinato, folk material, the ballet's scandal and modernism.

Exam technique (9MU0/03): Section A includes three set-work questions with audio and skeleton scores (short answers plus some multiple choice) and one short melody/rhythm completion exercise from an unfamiliar piece related to the set works' style. Essay one (Q5, 20 marks) links set-work study to an unfamiliar extract (audio, no score) - learn each set work's signature devices well enough to spot their cousins. Essay two (Q6, 30 marks) evaluates one set work as a whole with a score excerpt but no audio - structure by element, anchor to the music, judge.

💡 Worked Example

Exam-style: 'Elfman's Batman Returns shows that film music is built on leitmotif and harmonic colour rather than conventional melody.' With reference to the specified cues, evaluate this view. (10 marks)
Mark-scheme logic: Mark-scheme logic (AO3 + AO4): (1) Define leitmotif and apply: Penguin and Catwoman ideas recur and transform across 'Birth of a Penguin Parts I and II' and 'Batman vs the Circus'. (2) Harmonic colour: dark chromatic/minor harmony, tritone-related shifts, celesta and low-brass sonorities - cite moments. (3) Counter-evidence: Elfman's sweeping melodic writing (e.g. 'The Rise and Fall from Grace') shows memorable tunes do exist; Herrmann's Psycho by contrast abandons melody for ostinato and dissonance in 'The Murder', showing film music spans both poles. (4) Context: post-Wagnerian leitmotif tradition in Hollywood scoring; music's function is characterisation and cue-matching to picture. (5) Judgement: largely valid but overstated - leitmotif and colour dominate, yet melodic identity remains central to Elfman's idiom.

❓ Practice Questions & Mark Schemes

Describe how Bach presents the chorale melody in movement 1 of Ein feste Burg and explain the effect.
Answer: Movement 1 is a chorale fantasia: the chorale appears in augmentation - long notes - in the unison violins (with viola), while the bass voice sings the chorale in canon with the continuo; the remaining voices enter imitatively over independent, driving instrumental parts. Effect: the cantus firmus in long notes gives structural spine and symbolic weight (the Word of the Reformation hymn) while the surrounding imitation and busy continuo create energy and density. Terminology to use: chorale fantasia, augmentation, canon, cantus firmus, imitative entries, independent continuo.
How does Herrmann create tension in the Psycho cues without conventional melody?
Answer: String orchestra alone for uniform, close timbre; relentless ostinati (e.g. the 'Prelude' semiquaver pattern) that drive without resolution; high-register violin writing ('The Murder' stabs) exploiting the instrument's harshest register; dissonance - minor seconds, tritones, cluster chords - replacing functional harmony; short fragmentary gestures ('The Toys', 'The Cellar') mirroring filmic jump cuts; abrupt dynamic contrasts. Effect: anxiety from rhythm and timbre rather than tune - 'The Murder' is horror built on sound itself. Context: 1960, Hitchcock's black-and-white minimalism; Herrmann's string choice mirrors it.
Compare the use of non-Western musical material in Debussy's Pagodes and Shankar's Breathing Under Water.
Answer: Debussy (Pagodes, 1903): a Western composer absorbing Javanese gamelan second-hand (1889 Exposition Universelle) - pentatonic melody, layered ostinati, heterophonic textures, bell sonorities; harmony is evoked rather than functional; the piano imitates gamelan timbre. Shankar (2007): an Indian classical artist fusing her own tradition (raga melody, tanpura drone, tabla tala cycles, improvisation) with Western electronica and pop songwriting - loops, beats, English vocals. Judgement: Debussy's is imitation/translation of timbre and scale; Shankar's is genuine bicultural collaboration - both suspend conventional functional harmony, but Shankar retains tala/metrical frameworks while Debussy floats free.
Explain the significance of the rhythm and harmony in 'The Augurs of Spring' from The Rite of Spring.
Answer: The pounding chord - a dominant 7th (E-flat7) superimposed on a major triad (F-flat major) a semitone below - is struck with accented off-beats over an ostinato, so metre is disrupted by irregular accents: eight quavers grouped unevenly. Harmony is dissonant and static rather than functional - the shock is in the sound itself, not progression. Significance: primitivism - raw rhythmic energy and ritual violence (the ballet's sacrificial subject); the 1913 premiere riot; the passage redefined what rhythm and dissonance could do in Western art music, influencing 20th-century composition.
What makes Eleanor Rigby unusual as a 1960s pop recording, and how would you link it to another set work in the unfamiliar-music essay?
Answer: No conventional pop backing: no drums or guitar - a double string octet arranged by George Martin; Dorian-mode inflected verse (modal colour within a pop song); narrative lyric on urban loneliness (all the lonely people); through-composed-ish verses with refrain. Links for essay one (Q5): Vaughan Williams On Wenlock Edge - strings accompanying voice, word painting, English melancholy; Kate Bush's 'And dream of sheep' - voice and intimate accompaniment, isolation theme; Herrmann - string-only sonority and dark minor harmony. State the link (instrumentation/texture/mood) then compare treatment and context.
