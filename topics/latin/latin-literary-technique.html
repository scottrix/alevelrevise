Literary Technique and Essay Writing

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level OCR, WJEC/Eduqas

Latin literary technique: anaphora, tricolon, hyperbaton, chiasmus and more — analysing effect, with essay frames for the OCR H443 literature paper.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Recognise the major stylistic devices in Latin prose and verse
  • Analyse each device's effect on the reader/audience
  • Write Latin literature essays to the mark scheme
  • Use essay frames for technique-driven answers

📋 Key Definitions and Core Concepts

Anaphora: Repetition of a word or phrase at the start of successive clauses — emphasis through rhythm ('non... non... non...').
Tricolon: Three parallel phrases or clauses with increasing force — 'veni, vidi, vici' pattern; the rhetorical crescendo.
Hyperbaton: Separation of linked words (adjective from noun) for emphasis — the reader holds the pair apart until the line's end.
Chiasmus: A-B-B-A word order (crossing); balance with reversal — the ABBA pattern of emphasis.
Alliteration and assonance: Initial-consonant repetition (alliteration) and vowel-sound repetition (assonance) — sound patterning for effect.
Polysyndeton and asyndeton: Repeated conjunctions (polysyndeton: 'et... et... et...') for accumulation; their omission (asyndeton) for pace and force.
Zeugma: One verb governing multiple objects in different senses — economy and wit in a single construction.
Litotes: Affirmation by negating the opposite ('non ignoro' = I know very well) — understatement for emphasis.

Analyse the EFFECT, not just the label. The mark scheme pays for the device's contribution: 'the tricolon compresses Caesar's speed into three verbs, its asyndeton mirroring the pace' — label + pattern + effect. A device named without its effect is half a point.

Author styles: Cicero, Virgil, Ovid

Cicero (prose): the rhetorical period — tricolons, anaphora, balance (parallelism of clauses), the periodic build, rhetorical questions, ethopoeia (character portrayal); his courtroom speeches (In Verrem, Pro Milone) perform persuasion. Virgil (verse): elevated epic — golden lines, hyperbaton, enjambment, spondees at weight, the epic simile, the ekphrasis; his Aeneid marries Homer's models with Roman politics. Ovid (verse): witty, antithetical — zeugma, the couplet's pointe, the transformation's irony, rhetorical playfulness; his elegiac couplets (Amores) and hexameters (Metamorphoses) trade on surprise. Recognising the AUTHOR's style is a short cut: expect Cicero's balance, Virgil's suspension, Ovid's wit.

Essay frames

The technique essay (c. 20 marks, OCR): (1) thesis — the passage's style serves its theme (one sentence); (2) two or three analytical points: device + pattern + effect, with the Latin quoted; (3) the context (the author's purpose, the genre's conventions); (4) evaluation — the style's contribution. Example frame: 'The passage's anaphora (quote) hammers the theme of loss; the tricolon (quote) completes it; the asyndeton (quote) denies the reader rest — the style enacts the emotion the theme describes.'

Latinate analysis in English essays. The A-level essays are written in ENGLISH about the Latin: quote the Latin (short phrases), analyse in English, cite line numbers. The comprehension questions (the passage printed with notes) reward precise linguistic knowledge; the essay rewards the technique's analysis. Preparation: know each device's Latin pattern (the anaphora's repeated word, the tricolon's three units), so the recognition is instant.

💡 Worked Example

Exam-style: Analyse the style of the following passage (about 10 lines) and discuss how it contributes to the text's themes. [OCR H443 literature, 20 marks]
Mark-scheme logic: Frame for the marks: (1) identify the devices (the anaphora's repeated word, the tricolon's units, the chiasmus's crossing); (2) analyse each with the Latin quoted and its effect on the reader; (3) link the style to the theme (the style enacts the emotion); (4) evaluate (the author's purpose, the genre's conventions). ❓ Practice Questions & Mark Schemes

How would you analyse a tricolon in a Latin passage?
Answer: Identify the three parallel units (quote each); note their grammatical parallelism (same case/verb forms) and their rising force (the third usually longest or weightiest); analyse the effect (completeness, crescendo — 'veni, vidi, vici' compresses achievement into three verbs); link to the theme (the style enacts the argument's build). Quote the Latin and cite the line numbers.
What is the effect of asyndeton versus polysyndeton?
Answer: Asyndeton (no conjunctions): pace, force, breathlessness — events tumble without connection (Caesar's battle narratives); polysyndeton (repeated 'et... et... et...'): accumulation, abundance, the wealth of detail slowing the line. The contrast is the effect: an asyndetic list races; a polysyndetic one piles up. Analyse with the Latin quoted and the effect on the reader.
How does hyperbaton create emphasis in Virgil?
Answer: The separated adjective-noun pair ('magnanimum... Aenean' split across the line) holds the reader in suspension — the pair completes at the line's end, its reunion an aural resolution. The effect: emphasis on BOTH words (the adjective's weight before the noun arrives) and the epic's elevated pace. Link to the theme: the suspension enacts the epic's gravitas; the golden line (A-B-A-B) is its highest form.
How would you use zeugma in an analysis of Ovid?
Answer: Zeugma = one verb governing multiple objects in different senses — Ovid's wit lives there: 'the fire consumed the city and his patience' pattern (one verb, literal + figurative objects). Analyse: the economy (one construction, two meanings), the wit (the collision of senses), the theme (the transformation's irony). Quote the Latin; the zeugma's surprise is the couplet's pointe.
How should an essay analyse an author's style (Cicero vs Virgil vs Ovid)?
Answer: Expect the author's signature: Cicero's balance (tricolon, anaphora, periodic build), Virgil's suspension (hyperbaton, golden line, enjambment), Ovid's wit (zeugma, the couplet's pointe). Quote the Latin, analyse the device's pattern + effect, link to the theme and the genre's conventions, evaluate the style's contribution. The author-style short cut makes recognition instant — then the analysis earns the marks.
