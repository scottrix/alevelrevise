Set Texts

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level OCR, WJEC/Eduqas

OCR H443 set texts: Cicero's prose, Ovid's verse and Virgil's Aeneid — key lines, literary technique, context and set-text essay frames.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Know the set prose and verse texts and their contexts
  • Analyse key lines with literary technique and effect
  • Apply context (author, audience, purpose) to set-text analysis
  • Write set-text essays to the mark scheme: knowledge, analysis, evaluation

📋 Key Definitions and Core Concepts

Set text: The prescribed passage of Latin (prose and verse) examined in translation and analysis; OCR prescribes prose (Cicero/Ovid/Livy) and verse (Ovid/Virgil/Catullus) options.
Aeneid: Virgil's epic (12 books, c. 29-19 BC) on Aeneas' flight from Troy and the founding of Rome; the A-level verse set text (books prescribed per year).
Elegiac couplet: The Ovidian metre: hexameter + pentameter alternating; the elegy's form (Amores, Tristia, Metamorphoses-era usage).
Rhetorical period: Ciceronian prose architecture: the tricolon, the periodic sentence, the parallelism and balance of the courtroom speech.
Ekphrasis: A literary description of a work of art (Virgil's shield of Aeneas, Book 8); a set-text essay staple.

The set texts: prose and verse

Prose (OCR options, rotating by year): Cicero. The courtroom and political speeches (In Verrem, Pro Milone, the Philippics, In Catilinam): the rhetorical period's architecture — tricolon ('veni, vidi, vici' pattern), parallel clauses, the periodic suspension; the rhetorical questions; the moralising. Context: the Late Republic's courts (the senatorial jury, the bribery), Cicero's novus homo position, the speeches' publication (edited for readers, not verbatim). Verse (OCR options): Ovid. The Metamorphoses (mythological epics in hexameters) and the Amores/Tristia (elegiac couplets): witty, antithetical, the transformation's irony. Virgil's Aeneid. The epic's books (1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12 prescribed in rotation): Aeneas' piety (pius Aeneas), the Dido affair (Book 4's tragedy), the underworld (Book 6), the shield (Book 8's ekphrasis), the ending's fury (Book 12).

Learning the lines. For each set text: 5-8 key passages, each with (1) the Latin's technique (device + effect), (2) the context (what precedes/follows), (3) the translation's nuance. The A-level essays (c. 20 marks) ask for knowledge + analysis + evaluation: quote the Latin (or reference the line numbers), analyse the technique's effect, evaluate its contribution to the text's themes.

Literary technique in the set texts

In Virgil: the ekphrasis (Aeneas' shield, Book 8: Rome's future history in the shield's metal — the epic's themes in art); the similes (the hunt, the storm, the ants — epic similes as thematic commentary); the gods' machinery (Juno's opposition, Venus' aid — the divine plan vs human suffering); Dido's curse (Book 4 — the Punic Wars' aetiology); the ending (Aeneas' furor — Turnus' killing, the piety question). In Ovid: the transformation's wit (Daphne's laurel, the metamorphoses as irony and punishment); the antithesis and the couplet's balance; the narrator's playfulness. In Cicero: the tricolon and anaphora (the courtroom's rhythm); the rhetorical question (the jury's engagement); the ethopoeia (character portrayal — the opponent's vice); the periodic build (the sentence's suspense).

Essay frames. The set-text essay: (1) thesis (the passage's contribution to the theme); (2) two or three analytical points (technique + effect, with the Latin quoted or referenced); (3) context (the author's purpose, the audience); (4) evaluation (the passage's significance). The unseen-style technique (grammar + sense) applies to the SET text too — the essays reward precise linguistic knowledge.

Exam technique

The OCR set-text paper (Verse Literature / Prose Literature): comprehension questions on the passages (the text printed with notes) + an essay (c. 20 marks) on a theme or passage. Technique: (1) read the passage twice (the notes' glosses first); (2) answer the comprehension questions with the TEXT's evidence (quote the Latin); (3) the essay: thesis, technique + effect with the Latin, context, evaluation; (4) time: the comprehension first (marks per minute), the essay planned in the margin. The set texts reward preparation: 5-8 key passages per text, known for technique and context.

💡 Worked Example

Exam-style: Discuss how Virgil presents the theme of piety in the Aeneid, with reference to Book 2 and one other passage. [OCR H443 verse literature, 20 marks]
Mark-scheme logic: Knowledge: Book 2's fall of Troy (Aeneas' flight with Anchises and the Penates — the family and gods carried out); the piety of Book 4 (Aeneas' departure from Dido — the gods' command over love) and Book 6 (the underworld, Anchises' prophecy). Analysis: 'pius Aeneas' as the epic's epithet; the similes' presentation of Aeneas' duty; the contrast with Turnus' furor (Book 12's ending). Evaluation: Virgil's piety is both virtue and cost — Aeneas loses Dido, Creusa, Troy; the epic's Roman theme (duty over desire) built on the cost. Marks: knowledge (the books' content), analysis (the technique + effect), evaluation (the theme's complexity).

❓ Practice Questions & Mark Schemes

How does Cicero's prose style persuade in the courtroom speeches?
Answer: The rhetorical period's architecture: tricolons and parallel clauses build rhythm ('quid expectas?' patterns); the anaphora hammers the theme; rhetorical questions engage the jury; the periodic sentence withholds the main verb for suspense; ethopoeia (the opponent's vice portrayed) and the moralising conclusion. Context: the senatorial jury and the Late Republic's courts — the speeches were published (edited), so the style serves readers too. Quote the Latin for the technique + effect in essays.
How does Ovid's elegiac couplet shape his wit?
Answer: The hexameter + pentameter's alternation sets up expectation and punch: the hexameter's flow, the pentameter's twist — the antithesis and wit live in the couplet's second half (the 'pointe'). The Metamorphoses uses hexameters alone but keeps the balanced antithesis; the Amores' couplets make the love-elegy's irony structural. For essays: quote the couplet's second line's turn as the wit's evidence.
What is an ekphrasis and how does Book 8's shield work?
Answer: A literary description of a work of art. Book 8's shield of Aeneas (made by Vulcan): Rome's future history in the metal — Romulus and Remus, the Sabine women, the Gauls, Actium — the epic's theme (Rome's destiny) presented as art within the epic. The ekphrasis lets Virgil summarise the future (the Aeneid's prophecies) without leaving the story; essays should analyse the selection of scenes and the shield's effect (the destiny made visible).
How does Virgil present Dido in Book 4?
Answer: As tragedy: Dido's love (the wound imagery — 'uritur' = she burns), her abandonment (the gods' command), her curse (the Punic Wars' aetiology — Hannibal foretold), her death (the suicide as the epic's cost). The technique: the storm simile, the cave scene's irony (Juno's wedding), the sympathy built for Dido against Aeneas' duty. Essays: the piety vs love conflict; Dido as the epic's first casualty of Rome's destiny.
How should I prepare the set texts for the OCR paper?
Answer: For each text: 5-8 key passages known for (1) the Latin's technique (device + effect), (2) the context (what precedes/follows), (3) the translation's nuance. The paper: comprehension questions (quote the Latin) + a c. 20-mark essay (thesis, technique + effect, context, evaluation). Time the comprehension first; plan the essay in the margin; quote precisely.
