Homeostasis and the Nervous System

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level All Boards (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC, CCEA) AQA

A-Level Biology revision: Homeostasis and the Nervous System. Learning objectives, key points, worked examples and practice questions across AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC and CCEA.

📌 Key Points

Key Fact: Negative feedback: deviation -> correction (e.g. blood glucose, temp, water); Positive: amplifies (e.g. oxytocin in childbirth)
Key Fact: Kidney: cortex/medulla; nephron (Bowman's capsule, PCT, loop of Henle, DCT, collecting duct); ADH: increases water permeability of DCT/CD; Loop: counter-current multiplier creates medullary gradient
Key Fact: Neurones: sensory, relay, motor; Resting potential -70mV (Na⁺/K⁺ pump, K⁺ leak); Action potential: depolarisation (Na⁺ in), repolarisation (K⁺ out), refractory period
Key Fact: Synapse: Ca^2⁺ -> vesicle fusion -> neurotransmitter -> receptor -> depolarisation; Summation: temporal/spatial
Key Fact: Hormones: insulin (β-cells, lowers glucose), glucagon (α-cells, raises glucose), adrenaline (fight/flight), thyroxine (metabolic rate); Diabetes: type 1 (no insulin), type 2 (insulin resistance)
Key Fact: Thermoregulation: thermoreceptors (skin and hypothalamus) feed the hypothalamic thermoregulatory centre; Cooling: vasodilation, sweating, less metabolic activity; Warming: vasoconstriction, shivering, raised metabolism via thyroxine/adrenaline; Hypothermia below 35°C core temperature

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Explain homeostasis: set point, receptors, effectors, negative/positive feedback
  • Detail kidney structure and osmoregulation (ADH, loop of Henle)
  • Describe nervous system: CNS/PNS, neurone types, resting/action potential, synapses
  • Explain hormonal control: endocrine glands, insulin/glucagon, adrenaline, thyroxine
  • Understand temperature regulation: hypothalamus, vasodilation/constriction, sweating, shivering

💡 Worked Example

Exam-Style Question

Question: Describe how the loop of Henle creates a water potential gradient in the kidney medulla

Model Answer:

Descending limb: permeable to water, impermeable to ions -> water leaves by osmosis. Ascending limb: impermeable to water, active Na⁺/Cl⁻ transport out -> filtrate diluted, medulla concentrated. Counter-current multiplier: flow in opposite directions maintains gradient

Q1: Describe the sequence of events in an action potential.

Show Model Answer

Answer: Resting potential of −70 mV is maintained by the Na⁺/K⁺ pump (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in) and K⁺ leak channels. A stimulus depolarises the membrane to threshold (−55 mV), opening voltage-gated Na⁺ channels so Na⁺ floods in (depolarisation to about +40 mV). Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels then close and voltage-gated K⁺ channels open, so K⁺ leaves (repolarisation). Brief hyperpolarisation follows before the refractory period resets the channels; the resting potential is restored by the pump.

Q2: Explain how transmission occurs across a cholinergic synapse.

Show Model Answer

Answer: The action potential arrives at the presynaptic knob, opening voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels; Ca²⁺ enters and causes synaptic vesicles to fuse with the presynaptic membrane, releasing acetylcholine (ACh) by exocytosis. ACh diffuses across the cleft and binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, opening Na⁺ channels; depolarisation above threshold generates a new action potential. Acetylcholinesterase hydrolyses ACh into choline and ethanoic acid (choline is reabsorbed to reform ACh), preventing continuous stimulation.

Q3: Describe how insulin and glucagon control blood glucose concentration.

Show Model Answer

Answer: When blood glucose rises, β-cells of the islets of Langerhans secrete insulin, which increases glucose uptake via GLUT transporters, promotes glycogenesis (glucose → glycogen) in liver and muscle, raises respiration and converts excess glucose to fat. When glucose falls, α-cells secrete glucagon, which activates adenylyl cyclase and cAMP (second messenger), activating phosphorylase for glycogenolysis (glycogen → glucose) and gluconeogenesis. Both operate by negative feedback to return blood glucose to the set point (~90 mg/100 cm³).
