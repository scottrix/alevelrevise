B3: Respiration, Photosynthesis and Bioenergetics

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level All Boards (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC, CCEA) AQA

Glycolysis, Krebs cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, light-dependent and light-independent photosynthetic reactions.

📋 Key Definitions and Core Concepts

Chemiosmosis: ATP synthesis driven by proton electrochemical gradient across inner mitochondrial/thylakoid membrane through ATP synthase.
Photolysis: Light-dependent splitting of water in PSII: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂.
Glycolysis: Cytoplasmic pathway in which glucose is phosphorylated using 2 ATP, split into two triose phosphate molecules, then oxidised to pyruvate; net yield of 2 ATP, 2 NADH and 2 pyruvate per glucose.
Krebs Cycle: In the mitochondrial matrix, acetyl-CoA (2C) combines with oxaloacetate (4C) to form citrate (6C), which is decarboxylated and dehydrogenated in a series of reactions regenerating oxaloacetate; each turn yields 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP and 2 CO₂.
Electron Transport Chain: NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to electron carriers (complexes I–IV) on the inner mitochondrial membrane; energy released pumps H⁺ into the intermembrane space, generating the proton gradient used in chemiosmosis.
Light-Dependent Reaction: Occurs in the thylakoid membranes; chlorophyll in photosystems II and I absorbs light, exciting electrons that pass along an electron transport chain, producing ATP (photophosphorylation) and NADPH, with water as the electron source.

🔍 Key Principles & Specification Requirements

  • Glycolysis: glucose → 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP, 2 NADH (cytoplasm).
  • Krebs cycle: acetyl-CoA oxidized producing 2 CO₂, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP per turn (mitochondrial matrix).
  • Calvin cycle (stroma): RuBisCO fixes CO₂ to RuBP (5C) → 2 GP (3C) → reduced to 2 TP (3C) using ATP and NADPH.

💡 Worked Example Question

Exam-Style Question

Question:

Explain why oxygen is essential for aerobic ATP production.

Model Solution & Mark Scheme:

Oxygen acts as the terminal electron acceptor at complex IV of the electron transport chain. It combines with electrons and protons to form H₂O (½O₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂O), maintaining electron flow and proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

❓ Practice Questions & Mark Schemes

Q1: What happens to the Calvin cycle in prolonged darkness?

Show Model Answer

Answer: Without light-dependent ATP and NADPH, GP cannot be reduced to TP. GP accumulates while RuBP and TP levels drop, halting glucose synthesis.

Q2: Calculate the respiratory quotient (RQ) for tripalmitin C₅₁H₉₈O₆ + 145/2 O₂ → 51 CO₂ + 49 H₂O.

Show Model Answer

Answer: RQ = CO₂ produced / O₂ consumed = 51 / 72.5 = 0.70.

Q3: Outline the four stages of aerobic respiration and state the main products of each stage.

Show Model Answer

Answer: (1) Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose is split into 2 pyruvate; net 2 ATP and 2 NADH. (2) Link reaction (mitochondrial matrix): each pyruvate is oxidatively decarboxylated to acetyl-CoA; per glucose, 2 CO₂ and 2 NADH. (3) Krebs cycle (matrix): acetyl-CoA is oxidised; per glucose, 4 CO₂, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂ and 2 ATP. (4) Oxidative phosphorylation (inner membrane): electrons from the 10 NADH and 2 FADH₂ pass along the electron transport chain, pumping protons for chemiosmosis; oxygen accepts electrons and protons to form water, yielding about 34 ATP (roughly 38 ATP per glucose in total).

Q4: Describe the light-dependent reaction of photosynthesis and explain how ATP and NADPH are produced.

Show Model Answer

Answer: Light is absorbed by chlorophyll in photosystem II of the thylakoid membranes, exciting electrons to a higher energy level; they pass along a chain of electron carriers to photosystem I. Energy released by the carriers pumps H⁺ from the stroma into the thylakoid lumen, creating a proton gradient; H⁺ then flow back through ATP synthase into the stroma, driving photophosphorylation of ADP + Pₗ to ATP (chemiosmosis). Electrons lost from PSII are replaced by photolysis of water (2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂), and at PSI electrons, H⁺ and NADP combine to form NADPH for use in the light-independent (Calvin) cycle.
