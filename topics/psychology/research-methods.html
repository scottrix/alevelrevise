Research Methods

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level All Boards (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC, CCEA) AQA

A-Level Psychology revision: Research Methods. Learning objectives, key points, worked examples and practice questions across AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC and CCEA.

📌 Key Points

Key Fact: Experimental designs: independent measures, repeated measures, matched pairs; controls: standardisation, counterbalancing, random allocation
Key Fact: Sampling: random (unbiased), stratified (proportional subgroups), systematic (interval), opportunity (convenience), volunteer (self-select)
Key Fact: Descriptive: mean (parametric), median (skewed), mode (nominal), SD (spread), range; Inferential: t-test, Mann-Whitney, Wilcoxon, Chi-squared, Spearman, Pearson
Key Fact: Ethics: consent, deception (cost-benefit), debrief, withdrawal, confidentiality, protection from harm; BPS code
Key Fact: Reliability: consistency (test-retest, inter-rater, split-half); Validity: internal (IV->DV), external (generalisable), ecological (real-life), construct (measures concept)
Key Fact: Report structure: Abstract (150w), Intro (lit review, hypothesis), Method (design, sample, materials, procedure), Results (stats, tables), Discussion (interpret, eval, implications), Refs (APA)

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Design experiments: lab, field, natural, quasi; IV, DV, controls, counterbalancing, randomisation
  • Understand sampling: random, stratified, systematic, opportunity, volunteer; generalisability
  • Analyse data: descriptive (mean, median, mode, SD, range), inferential (parametric/non-parametric tests)
  • Understand ethics: BPS guidelines, informed consent, deception, debrief, right to withdraw, confidentiality
  • Evaluate reliability (test-retest, inter-rater) and validity (internal, external, ecological, construct)
  • Write psychological reports: abstract, intro, method, results, discussion, references (APA)

💡 Worked Example

Exam-Style Question

Question: Design an experiment to test if caffeine improves reaction time. Identify IV, DV, design, controls, sampling, ethics

Model Answer:

IV: caffeine (200mg vs placebo). DV: reaction time (ms) on computer task. Design: repeated measures (counterbalanced). Controls: same time of day, same task, blind. Sampling: volunteer students (opportunity). Ethics: consent, right to withdraw, debrief, medical screening for caffeine sensitivity

Q1: A researcher compares two independent groups using ordinal data. State which inferential test should be used and explain why. (4 marks)

Show Model Answer

Answer: The Mann-Whitney U test. Reasoning: it is a test of difference (not correlation or association), it is used with an independent measures (unrelated groups) design, and it is suitable for ordinal data because it is non-parametric -- it makes no assumption of a normal distribution. If the data were interval and normally distributed, an unrelated t-test would be used instead.

Q2: Explain counterbalancing as a way of controlling order effects in a repeated measures design. (4 marks)

Show Model Answer

Answer: Half the participants experience condition A first and then condition B, while the other half experience B first and then A (the ABBA technique). This spreads order effects -- practice, fatigue and boredom -- evenly across both conditions, so they cannot confound the effect of the independent variable. Counterbalancing increases internal validity while retaining the advantages of repeated measures: fewer participants are needed and participant variables are controlled because everyone does both conditions.

Q3: A researcher has collected ordinal data on hours of sleep and exam scores and wants to test for a relationship. Explain which test of correlation should be used and outline one limitation of correlational analysis. (6 marks)

Show Model Answer

Answer: Spearman's rho should be used because it is a test of correlation/association between two co-variables designed for ordinal data, whereas Pearson's r requires interval-level data and a normal distribution. The researcher states a null hypothesis of no relationship and compares the calculated rho to the critical value at the 5% significance level. Limitation: correlation does not establish cause and effect -- a third variable, such as motivation or ability, could explain why sleep and exam scores are related, and the direction of the relationship cannot be inferred from a correlation coefficient alone.
