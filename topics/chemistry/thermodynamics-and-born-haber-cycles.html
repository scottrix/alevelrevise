C1: Thermodynamics and Born-Haber Cycles

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level All Boards (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC, CCEA) AQA

Lattice enthalpy, Born-Haber cycles, entropy (ΔS), Gibbs free energy (ΔG = ΔH - TΔS), feasibility of reactions.

📋 Key Definitions and Core Concepts

Lattice Enthalpy: Enthalpy change when one mole of solid ionic lattice forms from gaseous ions under standard conditions (exothermic).
Gibbs Free Energy: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS. A reaction is thermodynamically feasible when ΔG ≤ 0.
Enthalpy of Atomisation: Enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous atoms forms from an element in its standard state under standard conditions, e.g. ½Cl₂(g) → Cl(g), ΔH_at = +121 kJ/mol (always endothermic).
Electron Affinity: First EA: enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous 1− ions forms from one mole of gaseous atoms (usually exothermic). Second EA is endothermic — energy is required to overcome repulsion when adding an electron to an already negative ion.
First Ionisation Energy: Enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous 1+ ions forms from one mole of gaseous atoms: Na(g) → Na⁺(g) + e⁻, ΔH = +496 kJ/mol (always endothermic).
Enthalpy of Solution: ΔH_sol = -ΔH_latt(formation) + ΣΔH_hyd. Dissolving is feasible when ΔH_sol is exothermic, or when TΔS outweighs an endothermic ΔH_sol; hydration enthalpy is more exothermic for smaller, higher-charge ions.

🔍 Key Principles & Specification Requirements

  • Born-Haber: ΔH_f = ΔH_at(metal) + IE(metal) + ΔH_at(non-metal) + EA(non-metal) + ΔH_latt.
  • Discrepancy between theoretical (ionic model) and experimental Born-Haber lattice enthalpies indicates covalent character due to polarization.
  • ΔG ≤ 0 temperature threshold: T = ΔH / ΔS (ensure units match: J/mol vs kJ/mol).

💡 Worked Example Question

Exam-Style Question

Question:

For CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g), ΔH = +178 kJ/mol, ΔS = +161 J/(K·mol). Calculate the temperature above which decomposition is feasible.

Model Solution & Mark Scheme:

Feasible when ΔG ≤ 0 => T ≥ ΔH / ΔS.
ΔH = 178,000 J/mol.
T = 178,000 / 161 = 1105.6 K (833°C).

❓ Practice Questions & Mark Schemes

Q1: Why is lattice enthalpy of MgO much more exothermic than NaCl?

Show Model Answer

Answer: Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ have higher charges (+2/-2 vs +1/-1) and smaller ionic radii, generating much stronger electrostatic forces.

Q2: Explain why dissolving ammonium nitrate in water is endothermic yet spontaneous.

Show Model Answer

Answer: ΔH > 0 but solid-to-aqueous dissolution greatly increases entropy (ΔS >> 0). At 298 K, TΔS > ΔH, making ΔG = ΔH - TΔS < 0.

Q3: In the Born-Haber cycle for KCl: ΔH_f(KCl) = -437 kJ/mol, ΔH_at(K) = +90 kJ/mol, IE₁(K) = +419 kJ/mol, ΔH_at(Cl) = +122 kJ/mol, EA₁(Cl) = -349 kJ/mol. Calculate the lattice enthalpy of formation of KCl.

Show Model Answer

Answer: By Hess's law: ΔH_f = ΔH_at(K) + IE₁(K) + ΔH_at(Cl) + EA₁(Cl) + ΔH_latt. So ΔH_latt = ΔH_f - (90 + 419 + 122 - 349) = -437 - 282 = -719 kJ/mol. This is close to the theoretical value calculated from the purely ionic model, indicating KCl has little covalent character.

Q4: For NH₄Cl(s) → NH₃(g) + HCl(g), ΔH = +176 kJ/mol and ΔS = +284 J/(K·mol). Calculate ΔG at 298 K and the temperature above which the decomposition becomes feasible.

Show Model Answer

Answer: Convert units first: ΔS = 284 J/(K·mol) = 0.284 kJ/(K·mol). ΔG = ΔH - TΔS = 176 - (298 × 0.284) = 176 - 84.6 = +91.4 kJ/mol, so the reaction is not feasible at 298 K. It becomes feasible when ΔG ≤ 0, i.e. T ≥ ΔH/ΔS = 176,000 / 284 = 620 K.
