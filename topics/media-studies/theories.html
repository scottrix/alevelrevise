Media Theories

Year 1 / ASYear 2 / A-Level OCR, WJEC/Eduqas

A-Level Media Studies revision on the full set of required theories (OCR H409; WJEC/Eduqas A680): Barthes, Todorov, Neale, Levi-Strauss, Baudrillard, Hall, Dyer, Mulvey, Butler, hooks, van Zoonen, Gauntlett, Gilroy, Jenkins, Shirky, Bandura, Gerbner, Curran and Seaton, Livingstone and Lunt, Hesmondhalgh - each applied to set products for A* essays.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Recall the full set of required theorists and locate each in the correct framework area (AO1)
  • Apply each theory to named set products with precise, specific claims (AO2)
  • Evaluate each theory's relevance and limitations against contemporary media (AO3)
  • Pair theories dialectically (effects vs activity, determinism vs agency) in extended answers (AO2/AO3)
  • Avoid name-dropping: use theory to drive analysis rather than decorate it (AO2)
  • Synthesise across the framework - representation, industries, audiences - in 25-mark essays (AO3)

📋 Key Definitions and Core Concepts

Theoretical framework: The specification's required structure: media language, representation, industries and audiences, each underpinned by mandatory theorists - the set of theories the exam can always ask you to apply.
Hegemony: Gramsci: dominant groups maintain power through consent rather than force; media naturalise ruling-class values as 'common sense', which is why ideology hides inside ordinary representation.
Simulacra and hyperreality: Baudrillard: in postmodernity signs refer to other signs rather than reality, producing a hyperreal condition 'more real than real' - the theory for curated, artificial and converged media.
Structuralism: Levi-Strauss: meaning is generated by underlying structures - especially binary oppositions - not by individual elements; the theory for exposing what a text quietly privileges.
Cultural industries: Hesmondhalgh: media are risk-managed businesses using genres, stars, franchises and 'independent' branding; their central contradiction is creativity organised for profit.
Textual poaching: Jenkins: fans appropriate and rework texts in participatory cultures - the theory for fandom, remix culture and audience productivity.
Oppositional gaze: bell hooks: Black female spectators, excluded by white-dominated male-gazed media, develop critical, interrogating spectatorship - theory for race, gender and spectator positioning.
End of the audience: Shirky (and Gauntlett): Web 2.0 dissolved the passive mass audience; people now make, share and connect as well as consume - the theory for audience productivity and digital participation.

📌 Key Points

The set list is an argument, not a syllabus: effects (Bandura, Gerbner) vs activity (Gauntlett, Jenkins, Shirky); determinism (Mulvey) vs agency (Gauntlett).
Apply theory to set products, not in a vacuum: every theory in this topic is examinable as 'apply to the set products you have studied'.
Evaluate relevance: pre-digital theories need updating (cultivation and the feed; reception and the algorithm).
Pair theories dialectically for 25-mark structure: thesis, antithesis, synthesis.
One theorist applied in depth beats four name-dropped.

The theoretical framework: the set theorists

Know the set as a map. Every board - OCR H409 and WJEC/Eduqas A680 - assesses the same DfE-mandated theorist set, organised by framework area. The exam can ask you to apply any of them to any set product, so the revision discipline is: theorist, one-line claim, one set-product application, one limitation. Memorise in clusters, not as a list.

The set list is an argument. Read it dialectically and revision halves in effort. Media language clusters from Barthes' semiotics to Baudrillard's hyperreality: signs first constructed, then detached from reality. Representation clusters from Hall's construction through the feminist/gender theories to Gauntlett's identity-as-resource: determinism softening into agency. Audiences cluster into two camps - effects (Bandura, Gerbner: media act on audiences) versus activity (Blumler and Katz, Jenkins, Shirky, Gauntlett: audiences use and produce media). Every 25-mark audience essay is essentially 'run that argument with set-product evidence'. Industries cluster around one concern - power (Curran and Seaton) - regulated imperfectly (Livingstone and Lunt) in businesses organised around risk and contradiction (Hesmondhalgh).

Hegemony (Gramsci) is the connective tissue across all four areas: dominant ideologies are maintained through consent manufactured as 'common sense', and media are the consent-manufacturing machinery. Barthes' myth is hegemony's mechanism at the level of the sign; Hall's stereotyping and Dyer's projection are its mechanism at the level of representation; Curran and Seaton's ownership critique is its mechanism at the level of industry; cultivation is its mechanism at the level of the audience. When an extended answer needs a synthesising conclusion, Gramsci's hegemony is the theory that ties the framework together - and examiners reward exactly that synthesis.

Two housekeeping facts that score AO1 marks. First, who is paired: Curran and Seaton, Livingstone and Lunt, Blumler and Katz are paired names - cite them together. Second, dates matter less than periods: Barthes 1950s-60s, Todorov 1969, Propp 1928, Neale 1980s, Hall 1973-97, Mulvey 1975, Butler 1990, hooks 1992, Gauntlett 2002-11, Jenkins 1992-2006, Shirky 2008-10, Bandura 1961-63, Gerbner 1970s, Curran and Seaton 1980s-2003, Livingstone and Lunt 2012, Hesmondhalgh 2007-19. The pattern is the point: most of the set was theorised before the internet, so 'evaluate relevance to contemporary media' is a live question for nearly every theorist - the single richest vein of AO3 marks on the paper.

The language theorists, expanded. Barthes (semiotics): the sign = signifier + signified; denotation (literal) to connotation (cultural) to myth (connotation naturalised as common sense); five narrative codes (hermeneutic, proairetic, semantic, symbolic, referential) and anchorage - the fullest toolkit on the list. Todorov (narratology): five-stage equilibrium model, applies at any scale from advert to series. Neale (genre): genres are 'instances of repetition and difference' - and the repetition is an industrial risk-management strategy, which makes Neale the bridge from language to industries. Levi-Strauss (structuralism): meaning from binary oppositions; whichever side a text privileges is its ideology. Baudrillard (postmodernism): simulacra - signs referring to signs - produce hyperreality; the theory for curated and converged media. Propp: 31 narrative functions across seven character spheres; his gendered defaults (passive princess) are the standard complication.

The representation theorists, expanded. Hall (representation): constitutive not reflective; stereotyping as power in language ('The Spectacle of the Other'). Dyer: the stereotype's three functions (ordering, shortcut, projection) and the star image as multimedia construction. Gilroy: postcolonial melancholia - imperial nostalgia sustaining racial hierarchy. hooks: the oppositional gaze; 'white supremacist capitalist patriarchy' as the compound structure. van Zoonen: gender as discourse, varying by context and culture. Butler: performativity - gender produced through repeated stylised acts, so media supply and normalise the scripts. Gauntlett: pick 'n' mix identity - media as a resource for constructing self, against every deterministic reading.

The industries theorists, expanded. Curran and Seaton ('Power Without Responsibility'): concentrated, profit-driven ownership limits variety, creativity and pluralism - media serve capital before citizens. Livingstone and Lunt: the regulation dilemma (protect vs enable) sharpened by convergence; protection migrating to media literacy and platform duties. Hesmondhalgh ('The Cultural Industries'): risk managed through genres, stars, franchises and 'independent' branding; the creativity/profit contradiction as the industry's central tension. Gramsci (hegemony) is the connective theory across all four areas: consent manufactured as common sense, with Barthes' myth, Hall's stereotyping and cultivation as its mechanisms at the levels of sign, representation and audience.

The audience theorists, expanded. Bandura: social learning - imitation with identification and reward (the Bobo doll experiments); the strongest evidence-based effects theory, with the standard limits (artificial lab, Gauntlett's methodological critique). Gerbner: cultivation - heavy viewing cultivates a screen-shaped worldview ('mean world syndrome'), on averages, across populations. Hall (reception): encoding/decoding - preferred, negotiated and oppositional readings from social positions. Blumler and Katz: uses and gratifications - surveillance, personal identity, relationships, diversion. Jenkins: fandom as textual poaching; convergence culture; participatory production. Shirky: the end of the audience - mass passive consumption dissolved into making, sharing and connecting. Gauntlett doubles here: identity resource and 'making is connecting'.

Revision strategy. Cluster, do not list: memorise in the four framework areas, each theorist as claim - application - limitation (one line each). Then drill the theory-product grid until pairings are reflexive, because the exam question is almost always 'apply to the set products you have studied' - and the A* candidate is the one for whom theory-to-product is a reflex, not a search.

Applying theories to the set products

Media language. Barthes: the Tide advert runs the full order of signification - a woman with laundry (denotation) connotes contented domestic femininity, naturalised as 1950s common sense (myth); the Mirror's colloquial headlines mythologise 'the ordinary Brit'. Todorov: McCain's 'We Are Family' compresses the five stages into a three-minute arc whose new equilibrium encodes inclusive family; Submarine's new equilibrium is ironic (Oliver learns controlling the narrative is not living one). Neale: Luther is genre as repetition and difference - procedural beats plus gothic hybridity; The Returned borrows horror iconography with grief-drama syntax. Levi-Strauss: The Returned refuses to resolve its living/dead binary - the refusal is its theme; Straight Outta Compton's authenticity/commercialisation opposition is its own deepest tension. Baudrillard: Zoella's channel is a simulacrum of intimacy - curated 'authenticity' whose real referent is other vlogs. Propp: in advertising the product is the donor, the consumer the hero; in Luther the villain becomes helper and the hero criminal - the binary collapse is also a representation claim.

Representation. Hall: the Tide advert's housewife is constructed, not reflected - and its contemporary audience decodes the construction with irony; the Mirror and Times prove constructedness comparatively (same day, two realities). Dyer: the stereotype's three functions (ordering, shortcut, projection) all operate in the Tide advert; charity-advert history (the passive victim) is projection in its purest form, which the WaterAid advert's agency-countertype resists without escaping. Gilroy: Straight Outta Compton restores historical context to Black counter-culture while raising Gilroy's core question - who profits from packaging Black anger as entertainment; postcolonial melancholia lives in nostalgic newspaper nation-building. Mulvey: the Tide woman is to-be-looked-at spectacle; van Zoonen: gender as discourse that has shifted (1950s domesticity vs WaterAid's agency); Butler: Zoella's femininity and Attitude's fluid identities are performed scripts - representation supplies and normalises the performance; hooks: the oppositional gaze interrogates who is spectacle and who profits, the sharpest question to put to Straight Outta Compton and Luther. Gauntlett: against every deterministic reading, audiences pick 'n' mix identity from media resources - Zoella is both the resource and the commodification of the resource.

Industries. Curran and Seaton: the Mirror (Reach) and The Times (News UK) are press concentration in the flesh - profit-driven ownership limiting pluralism; their logic migrates to platform gatekeepers in the streaming age. Hesmondhalgh: Submarine (Warp/Film4 - the subsidised indie) versus Straight Outta Compton (Universal - the risk-managed biopic with a brand subject) is the two-case proof of cultural-industry risk management and the creativity/profit contradiction. Livingstone and Lunt: the watershed protects linear TV but is bypassed on-demand; PEGI governs purchase but not live social play; the Online Safety Act 2023 is their diagnosis confirmed - regulation migrating from content to platform systems. The BBC (Radio 1 Breakfast Show) is the PSB case: licence-fee-funded distinctiveness, Ofcom-regulated, competing with Spotify - market failure logic under platform pressure.

Audiences. Bandura: the Bobo doll's social learning (imitation with identification and reward) underwrites video-game violence panics and PEGI's age logic; Gauntlett's critique - the lab cannot bear the leap to real-world harm - is the standard rebuttal. Gerbner: cultivation (mean world syndrome) applies most plausibly to sustained tabloid crime coverage; the streaming feed personalises what cultivation used to average. Hall's reception theory: Tide decoded with contemporary irony (oppositional), WaterAid met with negotiated readings questioning the charity gaze, The Returned decoded as prestige by niche audiences and dull by mass ones - readings are socially positioned, not random. Blumler and Katz: the Radio 1 Breakfast Show is a four-gratification bundle (Newsbeat surveillance, features diversion, Greg James para-social relationships, youth identity) - its distinctiveness is its gratification mix. Jenkins: Zoella's audience as participatory culture (fan accounts, comments, collective identity); fandom's industrial payoff in Luther's between-series speculation. Shirky: the 'end of the audience' - Zoella is an individual outperforming institutions in youth reach, proof of Gauntlett's 'make is connecting' and of the amateur/professional boundary dissolving.

The theory-product pairing grid

Barthes - Tide, Mirror. Todorov - McCain, Submarine. Neale - Luther, The Returned. Levi-Strauss - The Returned, SOC. Baudrillard - Zoella, adverts. Hall (representation) - Tide, Mirror/Times. Dyer - Tide, WaterAid. Gilroy - SOC, newspapers. Mulvey - Tide, GQ. van Zoonen - Tide vs WaterAid discourse. Butler - Zoella, Attitude. hooks - SOC, Luther. Gauntlett - Zoella, Attitude. Curran and Seaton - Mirror/Reach, Times/News UK. Livingstone and Lunt - watershed, OSA 2023, PEGI. Hesmondhalgh - Submarine vs SOC. Bandura - video game debates. Gerbner - tabloid crime coverage. Hall (reception) - Tide, WaterAid, The Returned. Blumler and Katz - Radio 1, Zoella. Jenkins - Zoella, Luther fandom. Shirky - Zoella.

The discipline that earns the marks: a theory is only useful when it produces a claim the product's surface does not already announce. 'Mulvey applies to the Tide advert' is description; 'the Tide advert constructs its woman as competent spectacle because her domestic expertise is the 1950s' only permitted performance of femininity - Mulvey's to-be-looked-at-ness with van Zoonen's period discourse' is analysis. Revise by building these specific sentences, not by reciting claims.

Theories applied to the newspaper set products. The Daily Mirror and The Times carry almost the whole framework: Barthes (colloquial headline register mythologising 'the ordinary Brit'; serif restraint mythologising authority), Hall (comparative constructedness - same day, two realities), Curran and Seaton (Reach and News UK press concentration, cross-title politics), Gerbner (sustained coverage cultivating anxieties the papers' politics harvest), Cohen (folk-devil construction in crime and migration coverage), Gilroy (melancholic nation-building), Livingstone and Lunt (IPSO's self-regulation as the gap their dilemma describes). One pair of set products, seven theories - which is why newspapers are the safest anchor for any theory essay: nearly every theorist has a precise, non-obvious application to them.

Theories applied to the audiovisual set products. Luther: Neale (hybridity), Levi-Strauss (law/criminality binary collapse), hooks (the audience's position relative to a Black male lead), the effects debate (vigilante violence, watershed context), Hall reception (negotiated readings questioning vigilante glamour). The Returned: Todorov violated (no repair), Levi-Strauss (living/dead unresolved), Neale (horror iconography, grief syntax), Hall reception (prestige vs 'boring' - cultural capital). Straight Outta Compton: Gilroy and hooks (who profits from packaging Black anger), Hesmondhalgh (biopic as risk management), Propp (the industry as false hero, the LAPD as institutional villain), Todorov (the biopic arc). Submarine: Todorov (ironic equilibrium), Butler (adolescence as performed script), Baudrillard (stylised postmodern surface), Hesmondhalgh (the subsidised indie). Tide, McCain and WaterAid: Barthes (myth), Dyer (stereotypes and countertypes), Mulvey and van Zoonen (gaze and discourse), Todorov (advert-scale arcs), Hall reception (decayed and negotiated readings).

Theories applied to the online set products. Zoella and Attitude carry the contemporary half of the list: Baudrillard (the simulacrum of curated intimacy), Butler (performed femininity normalised for millions; fluid identities as citational), van Zoonen (gender discourse in vlog form), Gauntlett (identity resource; making is connecting), Jenkins (participatory culture, fan accounts, collective identity), Shirky (the end of the audience - an individual outperforming institutions), Blumler and Katz (relationships and identity engineered into the vlog grammar), Hesmondhalgh (the commodification of self-representation). The pattern: the online set products are where pre-digital theories are tested - and where the 'relevance today' evaluation marks live.

Using the grid in essays. The pairing strategy: pick two theorists whose disagreement is the argument (Mulvey vs Gauntlett; Bandura vs Blumler and Katz; Curran and Seaton vs the platform counter-argument), or one theorist whose application to a product produces a claim the product's surface does not announce. The fit test: a theory applied well generates a specific, non-obvious sentence - 'Mulvey applies to the Tide advert' is description; 'the Tide advert constructs competent spectacle because domestic expertise was the 1950s' only permitted performance of femininity' is analysis. Revise by writing those sentences, one per theorist-product pair, and the grid becomes ammunition rather than a table.

A* technique: theory-driven extended answers

The 25-mark extended questions (OCR H409: 'How far do you agree...?', 'Evaluate the relevance of...'; Eduqas A680: 'Discuss...', synoptic essays) are marked on three things: application (theory used on set products), argument (a position taken and defended) and evaluation (limitations and counter-arguments). Name-dropping fails all three; the A* method is a theory-driven structure.

The A* paragraph architecture - thesis, application, complication, judgment. Open each paragraph with a claim ('Hall's reception theory best explains why the Tide advert's meaning has shifted'), apply it with a precise set-product example, complicate it (which audience? which social position? what does the theory miss?), and judge ('reception explains the shift better than Mulvey's determinism because the audience's ironic distance is itself historical knowledge'). Four moves, one paragraph, and the essay is simply six to eight of these chained by argument rather than topic.

Theory pairing strategies - the fastest route to evaluation marks. Pair within an area: Mulvey vs Gauntlett (determinism vs agency in gender reception); Bandura vs Blumler and Katz (effects vs active use); Curran and Seaton vs the digital counter-argument (concentrated ownership vs platform plurality). Pair across areas: Hall's representation plus reception (a contested representation produces oppositional readings); Hesmondhalgh plus Gilroy (who profits from representing struggle); Gramsci plus Barthes (myth as hegemony's mechanism); Livingstone and Lunt plus Bandura (regulation exists because effects anxieties become policy). The pairing is the argument: the set list was built so its theories argue with each other, and essays that stage the argument mark themselves.

The evaluation bank - memorise one limitation per theorist, chosen for usefulness: Barthes (semiotics understates audience agency and historical change), Todorov (built on folktales; breaks on unresolved and interactive narratives), Neale (genres are more fluid than the model implies), Baudrillard (explains everything and predicts nothing; audiences enjoy hyperreality knowingly), Hall (often reduced to a three-box checklist; readings are patterned, not individual), Dyer (stereotype analysis needs the power question - who controls production), Mulvey (heteronormative, pre-digital; the gaze has shifted - van Zoonen), Butler (performances can be reflexive, not just repeated - Gauntlett), hooks (critique risks demanding perfection of representation), Gauntlett (celebratory tone understates algorithmic capture of audience labour), Jenkins (fandom is a segment, not the audience; platforms capture fan labour), Shirky (the 'end of the audience' overstates - most make little and consume much), Bandura (artificial lab, doll-imitation not real violence), Gerbner (correlational; the shared mainstream he assumed is gone), Curran and Seaton (pre-digital; plurality has partly migrated to platforms), Livingstone and Lunt (their dilemma predates platform-scale gatekeeping), Hesmondhalgh (risk-management explains packaging, not hits), Blumler and Katz (explains selection, not effects; assumes rational choice). One line each, deployable in any essay.

Essay plans: the three guaranteed question types

1. 'How far do you agree that [theory] is useful for understanding [set products]?' - State the claim, apply to two products, evaluate with the limitation bank, conclude with a judgment calibrated to the question's 'how far'. 2. Audience debates ('media influence vs active audiences') - run Bandura/Gerbner against Blumler and Katz/Jenkins/Shirky with set-product evidence, land on the algorithmic feed as the contemporary test case. 3. Synoptic power essays - Gramsci's hegemony as the frame, ownership (Curran and Seaton) and representation (Hall, Dyer) as the evidence, regulation (Livingstone and Lunt) as the attempted answer.

Timing and craft. In OCR H409 Component 02 and the Eduqas synoptic essays, the 25-marker deserves roughly 35-40 minutes: five minutes planning pairings, twenty-five writing, five judging. Write topic sentences that are arguments (a marker reading only your first sentences should recover your whole thesis). Quote theorists by name and claim, not by page. Cite set products specifically - scene, page, campaign, edition - because specificity is what converts AO1 recall into AO2 application. And close every essay by answering the question asked, not the question you prepared: the conclusion is where the examiner checks whether the argument was yours.

Finally, the revision method that makes this stick: for each theorist, write one application sentence and one limitation sentence on an index card, and drill pairing until dialectical structure is reflexive. The set theorist list is the specification's own argument about the media - effects vs activity, determinism vs agency, power vs plurality - and the A* candidate is the one who can stage that argument, with set-product evidence, under timed conditions.

Worked essay plan 1: 'How useful is Hall's reception theory for understanding the set products you have studied?' Para 1: state the model (encode, position, decode; preferred/negotiated/oppositional). Para 2: apply - the Tide advert's decayed preferred reading (contemporary irony as a social position); the Mirror vs Times political decodings. Para 3: apply - WaterAid's negotiated readings (charity gaze questioned); The Returned's prestige decoding as cultural capital. Para 4: evaluate - the three-box reduction misreads the 1973 paper; readings are patterned, not individual; reception is now measurable data. Para 5: judgment - highly useful precisely because it explains patterned divergence; calibrate to 'how useful' with a qualifier (it explains positions, not effects - pair with Blumler and Katz for use).

Worked essay plan 2: 'Media audiences are active, not passive. How far do you agree?' Para 1: define the debate (effects camp vs activity camp). Para 2: the effects case at its strongest - Bandura (imitation with identification/reward), Gerbner (cumulative cultivation); evidence: video-game panics, news cultivation. Para 3: the activity case - Blumler and Katz (selection), Jenkins (fandom as production), Gauntlett (reflexive, make-and-share audiences); evidence: Zoella, the SOC meme generator. Para 4: evaluate - Gauntlett's methodological critique of effects research vs the genuinely concerning frontier (algorithmic feeds, the Online Safety Act's premise). Para 5: judgment - activity better explains everyday behaviour; effects claims remain live where children and regulation are concerned; the feed as the genuine contemporary test case.

Name-dropping vs analysis: the fix bank. Bad: 'Barthes' theory applies to the Tide advert.' Fixed: 'The Tide advert runs Barthes' order of signification - laundry denoted, domestic femininity connoted, the 1950s settlement naturalised as myth.' Bad: 'Hesmondhalgh says media industries are risky.' Fixed: 'Hesmondhalgh's risk-management explains the biopic: Straight Outta Compton packages a brand subject through a proven genre, sold by a major.' Bad: 'Livingstone and Lunt say regulation is hard.' Fixed: 'Livingstone and Lunt's dilemma is visible in the Online Safety Act: the state has migrated from policing content to policing platforms because convergence outflanked the watershed.' The fix is always the same: theory + specific product + non-obvious claim. Drill three fixes and the habit generalises.

Conclusion craft and timing. The conclusion is where the examiner checks whether the argument was yours: answer the question asked, calibrate the judgment to its terms ('how useful' needs a qualifier; 'how far' needs a measure; 'evaluate' needs a reasoned verdict), and close on the contemporary question wherever it exists - nearly every theorist on the list was formulated before the internet, so 'relevance today' is the richest evaluative vein. Timing: the 25-marker deserves 35-40 minutes - five planning pairings, twenty-five writing, five judging. Write topic sentences that are arguments (a marker reading only your first sentences should recover your thesis). And drill until dialectical structure - thesis, antithesis, synthesis - is reflexive, because the set theorist list is itself an argument, and the A* candidate is the one who can stage it.

Worked essay plan 3: 'Evaluate the relevance of two theories to the media industries you have studied.' Para 1: state the pairing (Curran and Seaton's power argument vs the platform counter-argument; or Hesmondhalgh plus Livingstone and Lunt). Para 2: apply - Mirror/Reach and Times/News UK as concentrated ownership with political consequences; Straight Outta Compton as Universal's risk-managed biopic; Submarine as the subsidised indie. Para 3: evaluate - digital distribution diversified voices even as ownership concentrated; platform gatekeepers re-concentrate attention (the logic migrates to algorithms). Para 4: apply the second theorist - Livingstone and Lunt: regulation migrating from content to platform systems (Online Safety Act 2023) as the state's answer to the new concentration. Para 5: judgment - both theories remain relevant, updated for the platform age; calibrate the verdict to the question's terms. The plan generalises: whichever pairing the question invites, the architecture is identical - state, apply to set products, evaluate against the digital counter-argument, land the judgment.

💡 Worked Example

Exam-style: Evaluate the relevance of two of the theories you have studied to understanding media products today. You should refer to the set products you have studied. [OCR H409 Component 02 style, 25]
Mark-scheme logic: Mark-scheme logic (AO2 application, AO3 evaluation of relevance, set-product specificity): (1) Choose a productive pairing - e.g. Bandura (effects) vs Gauntlett (activity). (2) Apply Bandura: social learning (imitation with identification/reward) underwrites video-game violence panics and PEGI's age logic; Luther's vigilante violence raises the copycat question. (3) Apply Gauntlett: audiences are reflexive users of media-as-resource - Zoella's audience picks 'n' mix identity; the Bobo doll's leap from lab imitation to real-world harm fails Gauntlett's methodological critique. (4) Evaluate relevance today: effects anxieties remain live (Online Safety Act 2023 as policy built on them), but activity evidence is stronger for contemporary behaviour - with the algorithmic feed as the genuine test case (personalised cultivation?). (5) Conclude with calibrated judgment: Bandura remains relevant where regulation and children are concerned; Gauntlett better explains everyday contemporary audience behaviour - relevance depends on the question asked.

❓ Practice Questions & Mark Schemes

How useful is Barthes' semiotics for understanding the set advertising and newspaper products you have studied? [25]
Answer: State: denotation, connotation, myth, anchorage. Apply: Tide - laundry (denotation) becomes domestic femininity (connotation), naturalised as 1950s common sense (myth); Mirror headlines mythologise 'the ordinary Brit'; WaterAid's voiceover anchors its imagery to empowerment, resisting the victim myth. Evaluate: semiotics exposes ideology at the level of the sign but understates audience agency (contemporary audiences decode Tide with irony - Hall's reception) and historical change (myths date). Conclude: highly useful for exposing naturalised ideology - myth is the concept that turns description into politics - provided it is paired with an audience theory.
How far do Curran and Seaton's ideas about media power apply to the media industries today? [25]
Answer: State: concentrated, profit-driven ownership produces limited variety and pluralism. Apply: Mirror/Reach and Times/News UK press concentration and shared news values; Straight Outta Compton as Universal-packaged counter-culture; Submarine/Warp as the subsidised indie alternative. Evaluate: pre-digital - digital distribution lowered barriers and diversified voices; but platform gatekeepers (Google, Meta, Netflix) re-concentrate power through algorithms, so the logic migrates rather than expires. Conclude: still the strongest lens on ownership concentration - updated to target algorithmic gatekeeping alongside press barons.
Compare Bandura's and Gauntlett's theories of audience. Which better explains contemporary audience behaviour? [25]
Answer: State both: Bandura - social learning, imitation with identification/reward (Bobo doll); Gauntlett - audiences as reflexive users, media as identity resource, critique of effects research. Apply: video-game panics and PEGI logic (Bandura); Zoella's participatory audience and pick 'n' mix identity (Gauntlett). Evaluate: Bandura's lab cannot bear the leap to real harm; Gauntlett's celebration understates algorithmic capture and passive consumption. Judge: Gauntlett better explains everyday contemporary behaviour; Bandura remains relevant where children, regulation and vulnerable audiences are concerned (Online Safety Act logic).
How useful are Butler's and hooks' theories for understanding representation of gender and ethnicity in the set products you have studied? [25]
Answer: State: Butler - gender performed through repeated stylised acts; hooks - oppositional gaze, white supremacist capitalist patriarchy. Apply: Zoella's performed femininity normalised for millions; Attitude's fluid identities as citational and breakable (Butler); Straight Outta Compton - who is spectacle, who profits (hooks); Luther's Black male lead interrogating the audience's position. Evaluate: Butler explains construction but not race - hooks supplies the missing dimension; both risk demanding perfection of representation. Conclude: the pairing is stronger than either alone - Butler on how gender is made, hooks on whose gaze it is made for.
Evaluate the usefulness of Livingstone and Lunt's theory of regulation in the digital age. Refer to media products you have studied. [25]
Answer: State: the dilemma - protect citizens vs enable choice - sharpened by convergence; protection shifting to media literacy. Apply: watershed meaningless on-demand; PEGI governs purchase not live social play; IPSO governs press not platforms; Online Safety Act 2023 shifts Ofcom to platform duties and age assurance - their diagnosis confirmed in legislation. Evaluate: prescient on convergence and the protection/choice trade-off; but their framework predates platform-scale gatekeeping, algorithmic amplification and AI-generated content. Conclude: highly useful as a diagnostic; the machinery of regulation has had to change around the dilemma they named.
